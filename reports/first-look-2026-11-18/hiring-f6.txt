Hiring efficiency review: offers, hires, time in stage and source quality

To: CFO
From: Head of Talent

Summary
- Of 751 unique candidates, 34 were hired, a hire rate of 4.5%.
- Measured only on candidates whose process has closed, the hire rate is 5.1%, from 668 closed candidates. The gap to the overall rate is the open pipeline, not a lower bar.
- Referrals drive the result. Their hire rate is 18.1%, against 2.3% for every other source.

Data and exclusions
- The export has 768 candidate rows. I excluded the 17 rows flagged as duplicates, so every figure uses the 751 remaining candidates.
- I found no test or spam rows: every candidate ID is unique, and every role, source and stage is a standard category.
- The file covers applications made in the export window only, so the figures describe that window and nothing earlier.
- The export has no recruiter field, so I cannot give the recruiter view you asked for. I report by role and by source instead.
- The file records only each candidate's current stage and the date it last changed. It cannot show the step at which a rejected candidate dropped out, so drop-out is shown by final outcome.

Offers
- 44 offers were decided, and 10 were declined, a decline rate of 22.7%.
- Account Executive declined 4 of 11 offers (36.4%). Backend Engineer declined 4 of 10 (40.0%).
- Support Specialist declined 2 of 9 (22.2%).
- Referral candidates declined 8 of 27 offers (29.6%).
- Data Analyst and Product Designer accepted all 14 of their offers.

Hiring by role
- Data Analyst: 8 hires, a hire rate of 6.8%, with a median of 22.5 days to hire.
- Product Designer: 6 hires, a hire rate of 5.6%, with a median of 21.5 days to hire.
- Account Executive: 7 hires, a hire rate of 4.4%, with a median of 27 days to hire.
- Support Specialist: 7 hires, a hire rate of 4.1%, with a median of 27 days to hire.
- Backend Engineer: 6 hires, a hire rate of 3.0%, with a median of 25 days to hire. It also has the highest withdrawal rate, at 15.2%.
- Across all hires, the median time from application to hire is 24 days. Referral hires are included in that figure.

Source quality
- LinkedIn brought 37.4% of candidates, and its hire rate is 2.1%.
- Careers page has a hire rate of 2.3%. Agency has a hire rate of 4.2%, and referrals made up 55.9% of all hires.
- Job board brought 50 candidates, no hires, and a withdrawal rate of 16.0%.
- Referral brought 105 candidates (14.0% of the total) and 19 hires, which is 7.8 times the hire rate of everyone else.
- Referral hires take a median of 24 days, the same as the 24 days for other hires. Referrals are not faster; they are simply more likely to be hired.
- Inside roles, the referral hire rate is 23.8% for Data Analyst, 20.8% for Account Executive, and 8.0% for Backend Engineer.

Time in stage
- 83 candidates are still open. Their median time in the current stage is 2 days, and the longest is 11 days.
- Candidates at Interview have a median of 4.5 days in stage. Candidates at Offer are few, so their timing should be read with care.

Drop-out
- Rejections are 70.8% of all candidates. The median time from application to rejection is 6 days, and 62.0% of rejections came within a week of applying.
- 92 candidates withdrew before any offer, with a median of 5 days from application to withdrawal.
- Cohort maturity matters. August applicants have a hire rate of 5.9%. September applicants have a hire rate of 5.5%, and their process is largely closed. Of 160 October applicants, 46.3% are still open and none are hired yet, so October should not be judged yet.

Recommendations
- Put the next sourcing budget into referrals first, and track referral hire rate by role.
- Stop paying for the job board until it produces a hire.
- Review offer terms for Account Executive and Backend Engineer, where declines are highest.
- Add a recruiter field and stage history to the next export, so the recruiter and funnel questions can be answered from data.
