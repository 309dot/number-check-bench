SUMMARY
The export holds 2,558 rows, opened from late July to mid-October. I removed 52 spam rows and 74 merged duplicates, leaving 2,434 real tickets. Of these, 726 breached SLA, a rate of 29.8%. Median first response is 6.4 hours. Only 46.9% of tickets have a CSAT answer, and 14.8% of those are Bad. All rates below use the cleaned set and the CSAT percentages use only tickets with a CSAT answer.

The main finding is that one agent, Theo, accounts for a large part of the gap between where we are and where we should be. The rest is a priority-handling problem that affects the whole team.

Excluding Theo, the other five agents combined show a breach rate of 24.9% and Bad CSAT of 12.1%. Theo's gap to that group is large, and his median first response of 10.8 hours compares with 6.0 hours for the rest.

SLA AND SATISFACTION
Breaching SLA hurts satisfaction. Among 352 breached tickets with a CSAT answer, 19.0% are Bad, compared with 12.9% on tickets that met SLA. CSAT coverage is low, so these figures carry some uncertainty.

BACKLOG AND CLOSURE
285 tickets have no close date. For tickets that were closed or solved, the mean time from open to close is 3.8 days. Resolution time does not differ much by agent, so Theo's problem is speed of first response and customer-rated quality, not final resolution. I recommend tracking open and pending tickets weekly so the backlog does not hide further breaches.

1. Theo: pair him with Hana, who has the lowest breach rate at 21.5%, and review his first-response queue habits, especially Normal and Low tickets. Review his chat transcripts, where Bad CSAT is the highest in the data.
2. Jonah: review Urgent handling and escalation routing, given the 65.0% Urgent breach rate.
3. Priya: her Bad CSAT of 15.4% is the highest outside Theo. On Phone it is 22.2% across 27 rated tickets, so start with a quality review of her calls.
4. Team: Normal tickets need a clearer SLA clock and queue ordering. Low tickets almost never breach, so effort can shift to Normal.
5. Measurement: raise the CSAT response rate before using it for individual performance decisions.
